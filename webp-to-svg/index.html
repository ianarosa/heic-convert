WebP to SVG,
for graphics, not photos.

WebP shows up as both web graphics and web photos, so the answer to "WebP to SVG?" depends entirely on which yours is. A flat WebP graphic — a logo, an icon, a symbol — traces into a crisp, scalable vector. A WebP photo does not. This traces in your browser, warns you if it spots a photo, and never uploads anything.

The short version

Tracing rebuilds an image out of flat vector shapes. When the WebP is already made of shapes — a logo, an icon, line art, a screenshot of a simple interface — that rebuild is clean, and the SVG scales to any size without blurring, usually at a tiny file size.

When the WebP is a photograph, there are no shapes to find, only gradients, so the tracer approximates it with thousands of paths: the SVG is blurry, posterised and often heavier than the WebP. That's not a quirk to tune around — it's what tracing a photo is. Keep the WebP, or convert it to JPG, for anything photographic.

The graphics that shine

  • Logos and wordmarks. A flat WebP logo becomes a scalable SVG for print, favicons or big banners.

The honest caveats

  • Photos vectorize badly. The main thing to know: a traced WebP photo is blurry, posterised and frequently larger than the WebP. The converter's photo-guard warns you when it detects one. It still lets you proceed — but it won't dress the result up as good.
  • Tracing is a re-drawing. Even flat art is approximated, so very fine detail and soft edges get simplified. For a clean logo that's the point; for something busy, check the output.
  • Detail vs size. The quality slider controls how many colours the trace follows — higher for fidelity and a bigger file, lower for a smaller, posterised one.
  • Nothing uploads, and you can check it. The tracer is pure JavaScript on your device. Watch the Network tab, or cut your Wi-Fi and trace anyway — it still works.

Questions people actually ask

My WebP is a photo — should I trace it?
No. A photo has no flat shapes to trace, so you get a large, soft, posterised file. Keep the WebP, or convert it to JPG. SVG is for graphics.
My WebP is a logo — will it trace well?
Yes. A flat, few-colour graphic traces into a crisp, scalable SVG with editable paths.
Is it a genuine vector?
Yes — real <path> shapes, not the WebP wrapped in an <image> tag, which is why it scales without blurring.
Does transparency survive?
Transparent regions are traced as their own areas, so a graphic on a transparent background stays usable. Hard edges trace more cleanly than feathered ones.
Is WebP to SVG free, with no limit?
Yes — no account, no daily cap, no file limit. It runs entirely on your device.

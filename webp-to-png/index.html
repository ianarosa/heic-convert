The short version

Convert WebP to PNG when the destination needs PNG — an older editor, a pipeline that only accepts it, a design tool that chokes on WebP — or when you want a lossless working copy you can edit and re-save without losing a little each time. PNG also keeps transparency, so a cut-out stays a cut-out.

The trade is size. PNG is lossless: it stores every pixel exactly and compresses nothing away photographically. On a photograph that makes the PNG far larger than the WebP — often many times over. On flat graphics, logos and screenshots the gap is small, and PNG is exactly the right tool.

When WebP to PNG is right

  • An editor or tool that requires PNG. Plenty of software still treats PNG as the universal lossless format and won't accept WebP. PNG makes the file usable there.
  • A working copy you'll keep editing. Because PNG is lossless, re-saving it doesn't degrade the image — the tenth save equals the first.
  • Anything with transparency headed somewhere fussy. A logo or icon with a see-through background keeps that background as a PNG.

When to think twice

  • It's a photograph and size matters. The PNG can balloon to many times the WebP for no visible gain. If you just need it to open somewhere, convert to JPG instead — far smaller.
  • You expect PNG to look better. It won't. Lossless preserves what the WebP already had; it can't recover detail the WebP discarded.

The honest caveats

  • A photo PNG is heavy. Lossless means no photographic compression, so a photograph saved as PNG can be many times the size of the WebP. That's the format doing its job, not a fault — but it's real, and worth knowing before you convert a big batch.
  • Lossless doesn't mean restored. PNG makes a perfect copy of what the WebP decoded to. The WebP already lost some detail when it was made, and PNG can't bring that back. It preserves; it can't recover.
  • Transparency is kept — that's the upside over JPG. Both WebP and PNG have an alpha channel, so cut-outs survive the trip.
  • Nothing uploads, and you can prove it. Open DevTools' Network tab, or switch off your Wi-Fi, and convert one. EXIF metadata is dropped in the re-encode; rotation is applied.

Questions people actually ask

Why is my PNG so much bigger than the WebP?
Because PNG is lossless and WebP isn't. PNG stores every pixel exactly, with no photographic compression, so a photo lands far heavier. On flat graphics the gap nearly vanishes.
Does the transparency survive?
Yes. WebP and PNG both support an alpha channel, so a transparent WebP stays transparent as a PNG. That's a key reason to pick PNG over JPG here.
Will the PNG look better than the WebP?
No. It's a faithful, larger copy of the same picture. Lossless preserves detail; it can't restore what the WebP already discarded.

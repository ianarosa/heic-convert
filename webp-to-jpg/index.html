The short version

You're almost certainly here because something rejected a WebP. Converting to JPG solves that in one step: JPG is the most widely readable image format there is, and every app, printer and website written in the last thirty years can open it.

Two things to know before you do. JPG is likely to be a touch larger than the WebP, since WebP is usually the more efficient of the two — you're spending a few bytes to buy compatibility. And JPG has no transparency: if the WebP had a see-through background, JPG fills it with solid white. Everywhere else, JPG is the safe answer.

The honest caveats

  • The JPG may be a little bigger. WebP is usually more efficient at a matched quality, so expect the JPG to weigh somewhat more. That's the price of a file that opens everywhere — and it's still a modest file for a photo.
  • Transparency turns white. WebP can carry an alpha channel; JPG can't. A transparent WebP background becomes solid white. Need it kept? Convert WebP to PNG instead.
  • Two lossy formats in a row. WebP already discarded some detail, and JPG discards a little more on top. At the default quality you won't see it; push the slider up if the source was already heavily compressed.
  • Nothing uploads, and you can prove it. Open DevTools' Network tab, or just switch off your Wi-Fi, and convert one. It works, because there was never a server involved. EXIF metadata is dropped in the re-encode; rotation is applied.

Questions people actually ask

Why does my software refuse WebP in the first place?
WebP is newer than a lot of installed software. Programs from before it caught on simply have no decoder for it. Converting to JPG sidesteps that entirely.
Is WebP to JPG free, with no limit?
Yes — no account, no daily cap, no file limit. It runs on your device, so the only ceiling is your device's memory. A whole folder at once is normal.
My WebP has a transparent background. What happens?
It's filled with white, because JPG can't store transparency. If you need it kept, convert to PNG instead — PNG keeps the alpha channel.
Are my files uploaded to convert them?
No. Turn off your Wi-Fi and convert one; it works, because there's nowhere for the file to go. The whole thing runs in this page.
Should I go back to WebP later for the web?
If the destination handles WebP and you want the smallest files, converting back to WebP is reasonable — just know each hop through a lossy format costs a little detail.

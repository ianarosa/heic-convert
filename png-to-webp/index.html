The short version

WebP is the format that gives you PNG's best trick — transparency — without PNG's weight. On a photograph or a detailed graphic, a WebP is typically much smaller than the same image as PNG, which is exactly why the web runs on it. The cut-out background survives; the file just gets lighter.

Two things to keep in mind. WebP opens in modern software only — current browsers and recent apps, not every dusty tool. And at the default quality it's lossy, so it's ideal for photos and web imagery; for a flat graphic you want pixel-exact, push the quality slider up or keep the PNG.

When PNG to WebP is the win

  • PNGs on a website. Heavy PNG graphics slow pages down. WebP keeps the look and the transparency while cutting the bytes — often sharply.
  • A folder of screenshots or exports. Design tools spit out big PNGs. Batch them to WebP and reclaim a lot of disk, transparency preserved.
  • Cut-outs and stickers for modern apps. You keep the see-through background and shed the weight — the best of both, as long as the target is recent.

The honest caveats

  • Modern software only. That's the real cost. If the file has to open on something older, keep the PNG or use JPG for that copy. For today's browsers and apps, WebP is safe.
  • Default WebP is lossy. A photograph loses a little detail you won't see. For a flat graphic that must stay pixel-exact, raise the quality slider or keep the lossless PNG.
  • Transparency is kept — this is the whole reason to pick WebP over JPG here. The alpha channel comes across intact.
  • Nothing uploads, and you can check it. Watch the Network tab, or switch off your Wi-Fi and convert anyway. EXIF metadata is dropped in the re-encode; rotation is applied.

Questions people actually ask

How much will I save?
It depends on the image, so no fixed number — but for photos and rich graphics WebP is usually far smaller than PNG. The tool measures the real saving for your file, and keeps your original if a re-encode would be bigger.
Will the transparency survive?
Yes. WebP supports an alpha channel like PNG, so a transparent PNG stays transparent as a WebP.
Who might not be able to open a WebP?
Some older desktop software and a few upload forms. Current browsers and modern apps are fine. If reach matters more than size, keep the PNG.

Yes — no account, no daily cap, no file limit. It runs on your device, so a folder at once is normal.

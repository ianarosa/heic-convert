Before you read any of this

We make a competing tool, and you should weigh these pages knowing that.

A converter reviewing its rivals has an obvious motive to make them look bad, so these pages are built to take that option away from us. Every factual claim is a verbatim quote from the company's own published terms, privacy policy or security page. Each quote links to the document it came from and carries the date we read it, so you can check the source yourself and see whether it has changed since. No paraphrase, no ellipsis doing quiet work.

Where a competitor does something well, we say so — CloudConvert's security page is a good one, FreeConvert's retention promise is a normal and reasonable arrangement, and for jobs we don't do at all, such as video or OCR'd searchable text, a cloud converter is simply the right answer and we say that too. None of these companies are villains, and server-side conversion is a legitimate way to build software. The argument we're making is narrower: that uploading should be a choice you make knowingly.

Terms change. If a quote no longer matches the source, the source is right and this page is stale — the read date on every citation is there so you can tell.

    Is heictojpg.com safe?

    A genuine product from Beamr Imaging Ltd. that uploads your photos, promises deletion within 48 hours, and asks you to grant unusually broad rights over what you send — all three quoted from its own Terms of Use.

  • Comparison

    Is FreeConvert safe?

    By its own published account, yes: uploads over HTTPS, automatic deletion after a few hours, a metered free tier. A normal, reasonable arrangement, with the usual cloud caveat attached.

  • Comparison

    Is CloudConvert safe?

    Immediate deletion, no permanent storage, isolated containers, encrypted transfer — on its own account a well-run cloud service, and the remaining question isn't about the server at all.

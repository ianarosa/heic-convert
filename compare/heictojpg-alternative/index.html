Seven ways to get JPGs out of your HEICs. Three of them upload your files, three of them don't, and one of them isn't a converter at all — it just fixes the problem that sent you looking. For several common jobs, the right answer isn't us.

The short answer

There isn't one best alternative — there's a best one per job. Need an unusual format or OCR'd, searchable text? Use a cloud converter. On a Mac with two files? Apple Photos already does it. Only need to view HEICs on Windows? You don't need a converter, you need an extension from the Store. Want JPG, PNG, WebP or PDF with no upload, no cap and no install? That's the browser-based option.

Our bias, up front: we make one of the seven options below, and obviously we like it. The options that aren't converters at all

Windows: HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store

Worth knowing whether you convert anything or not. Most people arrive here because Windows shows a blank icon where a photo should be — HEVC is patented, Microsoft ships Windows without the decoder, and sells it in the Store instead. Install the extension and Photos and File Explorer simply start working.

Two listings exist: a paid one, and a free one titled "HEVC Video Extensions from Device Manufacturer" which isn't available on every device. Prices and availability change, so check the Store rather than trusting a figure quoted on a page like this one.

The catch is what it doesn't fix. It makes HEICs open on your PC. It does nothing about the person you're emailing, the upload form that only takes JPG, or the old Android on the other end. Viewing and sharing are different problems. If yours is viewing, this is the whole story.

Mac: Apple Photos, which you already have

If you're on a Mac and you need a couple of JPGs, stop reading and use Photos. Select the photos, File → Export → Export Photos, pick JPEG. It's built in, it's free, it runs locally, nothing is uploaded, and it keeps more metadata than we do. For a handful of files there is no reason to open a website at all — including this one.

It's less convenient for a few hundred files from a phone that isn't plugged into that Mac, and there's no Windows equivalent. But for the common case, it's the right answer and it costs nothing.

Desktop viewers: IrfanView, XnView and friends

Long-standing Windows image viewers with batch conversion built in. Files stay on your machine, they'll chew through a folder of photos, and they handle formats a browser won't touch. The cost is that they're an installation — which is a real barrier on a work laptop where you don't have admin rights, and a big commitment for one folder of holiday photos. If you convert images regularly, they're a better long-term tool than any website.

The browser option — and what's wrong with it

This is ours, so here's the sales pitch in one line and then the complaints. Your browser can decode HEIC itself using a WebAssembly build of libheif, so conversion can happen inside the page: no upload, no account, no daily cap, no file limit beyond your device's memory, and it keeps working with the Wi-Fi off. Free, paid for by the ads.

Now the honest list, which is the only reason the rest of this page is worth anything:

  • No video, no exotic formats, no OCR. JPG, PNG, WebP and PDF, and that's the list. The cloud converters above beat us comfortably on breadth, and if that's your job they're the answer.
  • JPGs come out ~168% the size of your HEICs. Every converter on this page does that, ours included. It's the codecs: JPG's compression is from 1992, HEVC's from 2013.
  • EXIF metadata is dropped — dates, camera, GPS. Rotation is applied so photos are upright, but Apple Photos preserves more than we do.
  • Very large zips are limited by browser memory. The app warns you before you hit it, and saving files individually is steadier. A server has no such limit.
  • A ~1.4 MB decoder downloads once on first use — a network request, just not one with your photo in it.
  • We're a small independent project, not an audited company. CloudConvert has a security page and infrastructure.

How to check us, and everyone else. Press F12, open the Network tab, convert a photo: an uploader shows a request carrying megabytes, a local converter shows nothing. Or turn off your Wi-Fi and convert anyway — an uploader stops dead, a local one doesn't notice. That's your browser reporting on the page rather than the page reporting on itself, which is why it's the only test that counts. The ten-second version is here.

Questions people actually ask

What's the best alternative to heictojpg.com?
Depends on the job. An exotic format or searchable OCR text: CloudConvert. A Mac and two files: Apple Photos. Windows and you only need to see the photos: the HEVC Video Extensions from the Store. JPG, PNG, WebP or PDF with no upload, no cap and no install: a browser-based converter like this one. Anyone who tells you there's a single best tool is selling one.
Is there a HEIC converter that doesn't upload my photos?
Yes — this one, plus Apple Photos on a Mac and desktop viewers like IrfanView and XnView. The way to confirm any of them is to disconnect from the internet and convert anyway. If it works, the file didn't go anywhere, because there was nowhere for it to go.
How do I open HEIC files on Windows without converting?
Install the HEVC Video Extensions from the Microsoft Store. There's a paid listing and a free "from Device Manufacturer" listing that isn't available on all devices — check the Store for your machine. It fixes viewing on your PC, not sharing with anyone else.
Does iLoveIMG keep my files?
Its security page says it does not retain user documents, and that files are "automatically and permanently deleted within two hours of being processed", with HTTPS protecting data in transit and at rest. It's a Europe-based company, from the iLovePDF team in Barcelona.
Which one should I use for photos of documents?
Any option where the file never leaves your device: this converter, Apple Photos, or a desktop viewer. Not because the cloud services are untrustworthy — CloudConvert's stated practices are genuinely strong — but because a file that never travels raises no questions about what happened while it was away.
Why is my JPG bigger than the HEIC?
Because you asked for the older format on purpose. Around 168%, on every converter on this page. You're paying in megabytes for a file that opens everywhere, which is usually a good trade — it should just be a trade you know you're making.
